# Layout playbooks by use case

Pick the one that matches the page's job. Mixed pages: follow the playbook of the primary action.

## Landing page (persuade a stranger)
Mindset: skeptical, low attention, arrived from an ad or search. Pattern: Z on the first screen, layer-cake below.
1. **Hero**: what it is and the outcome in one line (under about 10 words), who it is for, one primary CTA, one visual of the real product.
2. **Proof** right after the hero: logos, a number, a short testimonial.
3. **Problem, then solution**: three benefits, each a front-loaded heading plus one sentence and a visual.
4. **How it works** in 3 steps.
5. **Objections**: pricing teaser, security, FAQ.
6. **Final CTA** repeating the primary action (serial position, peak-end).
Avoid: several competing CTAs, carousels (rarely seen past slide 1), vague headlines.

## Pricing page (help someone choose)
Mindset: interested, comparing. Hick's law and anchoring dominate.
- 3 plans side by side, one marked recommended. Annual and monthly toggle with savings stated.
- Rows in the same order across plans; differences highlighted, shared features grouped.
- What happens next under each button ("Start free, no card"). FAQ about billing below.

## Dashboard (monitor and act)
Mindset: returning expert, scanning for change. Spotted pattern.
- Top-left: the most important number or status. Answer "is everything OK?" in one glance.
- 4 to 7 key metrics with trend and comparison (vs last period), not raw totals alone.
- Exceptions first: alerts and anomalies rise to the top; normal stays quiet.
- Detail on demand: click through, filters persist, consistent time range across widgets.
- Density is fine for experts; consistency is what makes it readable.

## E-commerce product page (decide to buy)
- Above the fold: images (real, zoomable), name, price, key variant pickers, add-to-cart, delivery and return info.
- Reviews summary near price; full reviews lower. Specs in a scannable table.
- Sticky add-to-cart on mobile. Cart and checkout: no surprises in cost, guest checkout, progress steps.

## Docs and help (answer a question fast)
Mindset: has a specific problem, wants to leave quickly. Layer-cake and spotted patterns.
- Search prominent. Left nav for structure, right "on this page" for the current article.
- Each page starts with what it covers and the answer or the command. Copyable code blocks.
- Headings as questions or tasks ("Deploy to AWS"). Short sections, examples before theory.

## Form or signup (complete a task)
- Ask only what is needed now; defer the rest. Each field removed raises completion.
- Single column, labels above fields, inline validation after the user leaves a field, specific error messages next to the field.
- Long forms: steps with progress (Zeigarnik, goal-gradient). Save progress.
- The button says what happens ("Create account"), not "Submit".

## Onboarding (reach the first success)
- Get to the "aha" moment in as few steps as possible; the rest can wait.
- Checklists with progress; start one step already complete.
- Empty states that teach: show what goes here and a button to create the first one.

## Content or blog (read and trust)
- Readable column (45 to 75 characters), clear headings, summary at top, dates and author visible.
- Related content and one relevant CTA at the end, where intent peaks.

## Admin or internal tool (do repetitive work fast)
- Efficiency over persuasion: keyboard shortcuts, bulk actions, dense tables with sort and filter, remembered settings.
- Destructive actions confirmed and undoable.

## Mobile app screens
- Primary actions in the thumb zone (bottom), navigation in a bottom bar of 3 to 5 items.
- One task per screen. Gestures as shortcuts, never the only way.
